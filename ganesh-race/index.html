गणपति बाप्पा मोरया · Ganpati Bappa Morya

Why we celebrate Ganesh Chaturthi

the festival of Ganesha, who clears the way

  • Ganesha removes obstacles and blesses new beginnings. People pray to him first, before a journey, an exam or a wedding.
  • He is the son of Shiva and Parvati. The stories say Parvati shaped him with her own hands.
  • Ganesh Chaturthi falls on the fourth day (chaturthi) of the bright half of Bhadrapada, in August or September.
  • Families bring home a clay Ganesha for a day and a half, or five, seven or ten days. The festival ends on Anant Chaturdashi.
  • Modaks are his favourite: rice-flour dumplings filled with coconut and jaggery.
  • In the 1890s, Lokmanya Tilak helped turn a home festival into a public one, with street mandals that everyone could join.
  • At the end comes the visarjan. Ganesha is carried to the water with dhol-tasha drums, and everyone calls "Ganpati Bappa Morya, come back soon next year!"
  • Idols of plain, unpainted clay melt back into the water and are kind to rivers, lakes and the sea. Many families now immerse at home, in a bucket or a tank.
  • And the race says: the people who love us are our whole world.
