Why we celebrate Govardhan Puja

The day after Diwali, in many homes across North India

  • Families shape a small Govardhan hill on the floor (traditionally from cow dung), decorate it with flowers and lamps, and walk around it.
  • Homes and temples cook an Annakut, a "mountain of food", offer it to Krishna and then share it with everyone.
  • It remembers the story you just played: Krishna held up the hill for seven days so that the people and cows of Vraj stayed safe. That is why he is called Giridhari, "the one who holds the mountain".
  • The big idea: say thank you to the land, the rain and the animals that feed us, and keep each other safe in a storm.
